# Mean Reversion in Finance

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_reversion_(finance)
Fetched: 2026-08-21

## Definition

Mean reversion is the assumption that an asset's price will tend to converge to its average price over time.

## Core Concept

Identifies deviations from historical average prices: when current price falls below the historical average, the security appears undervalued (buy signal, expecting reversion upward); when it exceeds the average, downward movement is anticipated.

## Application in Trading

Two key components: identifying the trading range for a security, and computing the average price quantitatively (commonly a moving average over 50 or 100 days).

## Advantages Over Technical Analysis

Offers a more systematic approach than traditional charting — precise numerical values derived from historical data establish buy/sell points, rather than subjective interpretation.

## Important Limitations

- **Time horizon issues**: mean reversion may take years to manifest, limiting usefulness for short-term traders.
- **Structural changes**: new information can permanently alter valuations, making historical averages irrelevant (the mean itself moves).
- **Extreme events**: bankruptcy or a full trading halt can prevent any recovery to former price levels.

## Academic Perspective

Economist Jeremy Siegel distinguishes financial "mean reversion" from statistical "regression to the mean" — financial returns fluctuate considerably short-term but stabilize over longer periods, indicating predictable patterns rather than pure randomness.
