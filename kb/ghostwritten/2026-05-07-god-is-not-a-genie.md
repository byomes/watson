Somewhere along the way, a lot of Christians quietly picked up the idea that prayer is a mechanism. Say the right words. Say enough of them. Say them loudly enough, or long enough, or with the right formula — and God will move.

Jesus had a word for that. He called it babbling like the pagans.

In Matthew 6, He addresses the way people pray when they want to be seen praying, and then He addresses something equally problematic: the idea that the length or volume of your prayer changes what God does. In the first century, pagan worshippers would pray to their gods at length, believing the sheer quantity of words could compel a divine response — as if the god could be worn down, impressed, or obligated to act.

Jesus says don't do that. And His reason is clarifying: your Father already knows what you need before you ask.

Read that again slowly. God already knows.

Prayer is not a briefing session. You are not informing God of something He missed. You are not constructing an argument sophisticated enough to change His mind. The volume of your prayer does not equal its sincerity. The length of your prayer does not obligate His actions. This is not the gospel according to a pop star. God is not a genie, and you cannot rub the lamp long enough to make Him do what you want.

So what is prayer, then?

Jesus answers that with a model — not a mantra. The Lord's Prayer is not a script to be repeated until it works. Jesus didn't even speak English. What He gave His disciples was a framework: a way to organize the posture of your heart toward God. It begins with worship — not your request list, not your grievances, but the deliberate act of setting God apart as holy. Then it moves to kingdom priority: your kingdom come, your will be done. You are not asking God to align with your agenda. You are aligning yourself with His. Then provision, protection, and the acknowledgment that you carry the responsibility to extend to others what God extends to you.

That is a model for how a Kingdom citizen approaches the God of the universe. Not a transaction. Not a performance. Not a formula for getting what you want.

And here's the uncomfortable part of this: if the only time you pray is when other people are listening, Jesus has something to say to you. Private prayer — the kind that happens in your car, alone, before anyone else is awake — is the real measure of your prayer life. That's where you find out who you actually believe God is. If you only pray in front of people, the question worth sitting with is: who are you actually talking to?

God is not impressed by eloquence. He is not moved by volume. He does not reward repetition.

He rewards relationship.

Close the door. Pray to your Father who is unseen. He sees what is done in secret, and He rewards it.
